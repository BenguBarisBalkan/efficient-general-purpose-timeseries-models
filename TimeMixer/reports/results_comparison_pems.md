# PEMS multivariate short-term forecasting — SparseTSF and TimeMixer

Four traffic-network datasets (PEMS03/04/07/08), input **96 → predict 12**, metrics **MAE / MAPE /
RMSE** on the original (inverse-transformed) scale. Lower is better. Generated by
`benchmarks/run_pems_benchmarks.py` (idempotent).

- Config: `use_norm=0` and `task_name=long_term_forecast` (both follow the upstream PEMS scripts —
  the short-term exp class is M4-specific). SparseTSF: `period_len=12` (= 1 hour of 5-min data; it
  must divide both `seq_len=96` and `pred_len=12`), `lr=0.01`, 10 epochs, batch 16.
- TimeMixer: copied verbatim from upstream `scripts/short_term_forecast/PEMS/TimeMixer.sh` (channel
  mixing on, 5 layers, d_model 128, `lr=0.003`, 10 epochs, patience 10, batch 32).
- **SparseTSF and TimeMixer are both run locally**, same machine. The local TimeMixer reproduces its
  published average (MAE 17.51 against 17.41), so the local comparison is sound: SparseTSF's MAE is
  95% higher (34.13 against 17.51), while TimeMixer trains in about twice SparseTSF's time (1,404 s
  against 666 s over the four datasets). TimeMixer++ is the *published* average (TimeMixer++ paper,
  ICLR'25, Table 3), in italics.
- MAPE comes from `utils/metrics.py`, which zeroes relative errors > 5 before averaging; reported
  here as a percentage.
